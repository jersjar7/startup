# PE water resources opens a narrow, real lane

The PE Civil: Water Resources and Environmental (WRE) exam is now a single 80-question, depth-only, closed-book computer exam that anyone can book year-round for $400, and it is the largest and fastest-growing of the five PE Civil exams, with about **5,700 attempts in the fiscal year ending September 2025 and roughly 6,600 in the latest twelve months**. Jerson can register with NCEES and sit it as soon as he is ready, because Washington stopped requiring board approval on August 1, 2026, but the license itself waits on experience and lands around **mid 2029** in both Washington and Oregon. Prep is a $2M to $10M a year niche for WRE alone, mostly paid by employers through caps of $1,000 to $1,500, and it is split between $950 to $3,800 courses and $36 to $165 static practice exams, with almost nothing in between. That empty middle, plus an April 2027 change that grows the WRE reference list from two documents to seven and forces every vendor to update, is a genuine opening for an FE for Raccoons style product: a free, handbook-referenced problem bank with a cheap, realistic timed simulation. The honest catch is size and cost. At FE-style prices the ceiling is tens of thousands of dollars a year, not hundreds, and PE-level problems are far more expensive to author than FE problems. The product is worth building only as a focused simulation-first wedge, priced above the FE sim, timed to April 2027, and written alongside Jerson's own preparation.

## An 80-question depth exam where a quarter is not water

The exam is computer-based at Pearson VUE centers year-round, with **80 questions in a 9-hour appointment that includes 8 hours of exam time** and an optional 50-minute break ([NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)). That works out to about 6 minutes per question. The exam splits into two halves: after roughly 40 questions the examinee must submit them and loses access, so the practical plan is about 4 hours per half ([NCEES Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)). Questions mix multiple choice with "alternative item types": multiple correct answers, point and click, drag and drop, and numeric fill-in. Every item is right or wrong with no partial credit and no penalty for guessing, and a few unscored trial questions are hidden in the set ([Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)). Any practice exam that is pure multiple choice is therefore training for a slightly different test.

The old format with a shared 40-question breadth section and a 40-question depth section ended in April 2024, when all five PE Civil exams became depth-only ([ASCE Source](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2024/03/14/get-ready-for-an-all-new-pe-exam-format-heres-what-you-need-to-know)). The general civil material did not disappear, though. It was folded into the WRE outline as its own knowledge areas ([WRE spec, April 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)). The table below lists NCEES's twelve areas and question ranges, plus an estimated typical count found by scaling the range midpoints to 80 (the estimate is mine, not NCEES's).

| Knowledge area | NCEES range | Estimated typical count |
|---|---|---|
| Project Sitework (grading, erosion control, safety, curves, retaining walls) | 9-14 | about 10 |
| Hydrology (rational and SCS methods, hydrographs, IDF, stormwater ponds) | 8-12 | about 9 |
| Hydraulics, closed conduit (energy equation, pipe losses, pumps, networks) | 7-11 | about 8 |
| Hydraulics, open channel (channels, culverts, inlets, energy dissipation) | 7-11 | about 8 |
| Wastewater collection and treatment | 7-11 | about 8 |
| Analysis and Design (mass balance, hydraulic and solids loading, flow measurement) | 6-9 | about 7 |
| Drinking water distribution and treatment | 6-9 | about 7 |
| Surface water and groundwater quality (oxygen sag, TMDL, contaminants) | 5-8 | about 6 |
| Project Planning (take-offs, cost, schedule, economics) | 4-6 | about 4.5 |
| Materials (soil classification, concrete, pipe materials) | 4-6 | about 4.5 |
| Groundwater and wells | 4-6 | about 4.5 |
| Soil Mechanics (earth pressure, consolidation, bearing, slopes) | 3-5 | about 3.5 |

Grouped, **hydraulics and hydrology make up about 36 of the 80 questions, treatment and water quality about 21, and general civil content about 23**. For Jerson that is good news: drainage work at Kiewit lines up directly with the single heaviest block, and Project Sitework, the largest single area, is the construction-site content a Kiewit engineer sees daily. For a product, it means the "non-water" quarter of the exam is a real share of the score and cannot be skipped.

Scoring is pass or fail, results arrive in 7 to 10 days, and the passing score is never published. Failing candidates get a diagnostic that rates each of the twelve areas on a 0 to 15 scale against the average passer ([Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)). Retakes are limited to one per calendar quarter and three in any twelve months. WRE has the best pass rates of the five Civil exams: **71% of first-time takers and 49% of repeat takers passed** in January to June 2026 ([NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)). Roughly three in ten first-timers fail, and a failed candidate's odds on the next try are close to a coin flip.

## April 2027 swaps the reference shelf, not the outline

The exam is closed book. The only references on screen are the searchable NCEES PE Civil Reference Handbook plus the design standards listed on the last page of the specification, and solutions to standards-based questions are scored only against the edition named there ([WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)). NCEES provides the standards only during the exam, so candidates must buy their own copies to study ([Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)). The handbook itself is free through MyNCEES ([NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)). Its current version number could not be confirmed on an NCEES page; a copy hosted by Ohio DOT is marked Version 2.0 from October 2023 ([Ohio DOT copy](https://ftp.dot.state.oh.us/pub/Districts/D08/twebste1/PE-civ-handbook-2-0-1.pdf)).

The April 2027 change is narrower than the vendor marketing suggests. A text comparison of the current specification and the one NCEES posted in September 2026 shows **the twelve knowledge areas and question ranges are identical word for word; only the design standards page changes** ([WRE spec 2027](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf)). Through March 31, 2027, WRE examinees use two documents, the Great Lakes-Upper Mississippi "Ten States Standards" for wastewater (2014) and for water works (2018). From April 1, 2027 they use seven: OSHA construction rules (29 CFR 1926, including excavation and fall protection), the Corps of Engineers slope stability manual, FHWA's culvert design manual (HDS-5) and energy dissipator manual (HEC-14), the 2014 wastewater standards, the water works standards updated to the **2022** edition, and the military dewatering manual UFC 3-220-05 ([WRE spec 2027](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf)). PPI describes WRE as getting "the largest expansion of references" in the update ([PPI2Pass](https://ppi2pass.com/resources/pe-exam/pe-civil-exam-changes)). HEC-22, the urban drainage manual Jerson likely uses at work, is on neither list.

Two practical consequences follow. A candidate sitting in January to March 2027 has far less reference material to learn than one sitting after April. And because NCEES says specifications change only every 5 to 7 years ([NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)), a product built on this outline should stay valid until roughly 2029 to 2031, with only the standards-based questions needing edition tags.

## Jerson can sit any quarter, but the license waits until mid 2029

Washington decoupled the exam from experience on August 1, 2026. The board no longer accepts exam applications, candidates register directly with NCEES, and the board explicitly says not to apply to it until the exams are passed and experience is complete ([BRPELS, Exam Changes Coming](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2026/exam-changes-coming); [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)). Jerson can therefore open MyNCEES today, choose PE Civil: Water Resources and Environmental with Washington as his board, pay $400 and book a seat. Passing early costs him nothing under the new rules; the result simply waits in his NCEES record.

The license is the slow part. Washington requires 8 years of combined education and experience, with an ABET bachelor's counting as 4. Credit for a master's is now entirely at the board's discretion, and the former one-year cap was removed rather than replaced with a number ([WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)). Assuming the board grants the traditional one year for the BYU MS, he needs about 3 years of supervised work from May 2026, which reaches **about May 2029**, verified by a PE who directly supervised him at Kiewit. That PE does not need a Washington license, only one from an approved jurisdiction. Washington adds a $65 application, a $5 license print fee and an open-book law review form with an 80% pass mark, and it issues licenses in the first week of each month, so the realistic license date is **June or July 2029** ([BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319); [BRPELS fees](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/fees)). An EIT certificate is optional in Washington and does not change the path ([BRPELS EIT](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/engineering/engineer-training-certificate)).

Oregon gives the same date with more certainty. Its rule requires an accredited bachelor's plus 4 years of supervised experience, and a graduate engineering degree is written in as replacing one year, so 3 years of Kiewit work again lands around May 2029 ([OAR 820-010-1020](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_820-010-1020)). Oregon asks for heavier paperwork: 5 references, at least 3 of them licensed PEs, though the NCEES Record can supply them ([OAR 820-010-1000](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_820-010-1000)). One NCEES Record, transmitted to both boards, covers both licenses.

Only two things could pull the date earlier: engineering work done under a PE before May 2026 (an internship or a research role during the MS) and a more generous Washington credit for the MS. Neither is known from the notes. The timing of the exam sitting, by contrast, is a free choice. Since the license is gated by experience regardless, the best case for sitting in the **January to March 2027 window** is the lighter two-document reference list and his recent coursework. The counterweight is that NCEES finds pass rates peak among examinees about four years after graduation ([NCEES Squared 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Squared_access.pdf)), which argues against rushing an unprepared attempt. A failed March attempt would push the retake into the seven-document era. The notes found no Kiewit-specific reimbursement policy, so the amount Kiewit will cover needs to be confirmed with HR or his supervisor before he buys anything.

## About 6,600 attempts a year, mostly paid for by employers

NCEES publishes counts, and they show WRE as the biggest and fastest-growing PE Civil depth. WRE attempts rose from **4,276 to 5,486 to 5,697** over fiscal years 2023 through 2025, a 33% rise in two years, and its share of all PE Civil attempts climbed from 27% to 30%. In fiscal 2025 that comprised 4,313 first-time and 1,384 repeat attempts, ahead of Transportation's 5,460 ([NCEES Squared 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Squared_access.pdf); [Squared 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Squared-2024_pages.pdf); [Squared 2023](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Squared-2023_spreads.pdf)). The two latest six-month windows add up to about **6,571 attempts from July 2025 to June 2026**, roughly 15% above the prior fiscal year ([NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/pe-exam/civil/)). Because some people test twice a year, unique candidates are probably closer to 5,000 to 5,500.

The FE side shows why this audience connects to FE for Raccoons. FE Civil had 16,639 first-time and 9,863 repeat attempts in fiscal 2025, which produces roughly 13,000 passes a year, while PE Civil saw about 13,200 first-time takers ([Squared 2025](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-Squared_access.pdf)). That rough match implies most FE Civil passers eventually sit a PE, about four years later. Total FE volume grew 21% in two years, which points to PE Civil volume rising later in the decade. The catch for FE for Raccoons is the lag: today's FE users become PE buyers around 2030, so they are a future pipeline rather than a launch audience.

The money pool is modest. Prep prices run from a $59.95 NCEES practice exam to PPI's $3,795 tutoring bundle. Assuming each attempt carries between $300 and $1,500 of prep spending, WRE prep comes to roughly **$2M to $10M a year**, all PE Civil to about $6M to $28M, and all PE disciplines to about $9M to $45M (these are estimates built from NCEES counts and published prices, since no source measures the share of candidates who buy a course). Engineers have strong reason to pay. ASCE's 2026 salary survey puts the median income of PE holders **nearly $49,000 a year above** those with no license, though part of that gap reflects licensees being older ([ASCE 2026](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2026/10/08/civil-engineering-salaries-continue-to-rise-as-industry-evolves)). Against a raise that size, any prep price is small.

Employers usually foot the bill. The only systematic survey found, from Zweig Group and reported in 2015, says 89% of design and consulting firms pay PE exam and registration fees and 47% give a raise or bonus for passing ([NSPE](https://www.nspe.org/node/11703)). Prep courses are typically covered up to a cap: Kimley-Horn allows **$1,500 per employee for life** and buys School of PE seats at a negotiated $800 ([School of PE, Kimley-Horn](https://schoolofpe.com/pages/kimley-horn-registration)), and the City of Los Angeles reimburses up to $1,000 a year ([LA Bureau of Engineering](https://dpw.lacity.gov/bureau-engineering-employee-development-opportunities)). Both large vendors run sales-led corporate programs with unpublished per-seat pricing; School of PE claims more than 200 firm partners and half of the ENR Top 100 ([School of PE partnerships](https://partnerships.schoolofpe.com/engineering-firm-pe-exam-prep); [PPI Businesses](https://ppi2pass.com/businesses)). Two things follow for pricing. A $35 to $49 product is so far under these caps that many buyers will simply pay by card without filing for reimbursement, and the cap structure leaves room to charge two or three times that without anyone feeling it.

## Competitors leave a hole between $60 PDFs and $700 bundles

The market is a barbell. At the top sit full courses: PPI's live online bundle at $2,595 and its recorded course at $1,595 for three months, School of PE's live course around $1,700 to $2,200 depending on the sale, Civil Engineering Academy at $1,397, EET at $950 to $1,250, and ASCE's on-demand course at $595 to $695 ([PPI WRE](https://ppi2pass.com/pe-exam/civil/water-resources-environmental-depth); [CastorPrep roundup](https://castorprep.com/resources/pe-review-courses); [CEA](https://courses.civilengineeringacademy.com/p/the-ultimate-civil-pe-water-resources-review-course); [ASCE](https://mylearning.asce.org/diweb/catalog/item?id=14502295)). At the bottom sit static practice exams: NCEES's own 80-question PDF for $59.95, School of PE's workbook for $59, Engineering Design Resources' exam for $67, PE Prepared paperbacks for about $36 each, and PPI's practice exam book for $165 ([NCEES](https://account.ncees.org/exam-prep/471); [School of PE workbook](https://schoolofpe.com/products/pe-civil-water-resources-environmental-workbook-copy); [EDR](https://cew.engineeringdesignresources.com/product-category/pe-exam/)).

What is missing in the middle is exactly what FE for Raccoons already sells for the FE. **The large question banks and the realistic timed simulators are all locked inside bundles**: PPI's 550-plus problems come only in its $695 Learning Hub, and CEA's "same look and feel" CBT simulator only inside the $1,397 course ([PPI WRE](https://ppi2pass.com/pe-exam/civil/water-resources-environmental-depth); [CEA](https://courses.civilengineeringacademy.com/p/the-ultimate-civil-pe-water-resources-review-course)). The cheapest full-length realistic exam, NCEES's, is a PDF, not a timed on-screen test. Several books look built for the old format: PPI's practice book still contains two 40-question exams, a holdover from the breadth and depth split, and one buyer called it "useless for the WRE exam" ([thebookco](https://www.thebookco.com/ppi-water-resources-and-environmental-depth-practice-exams-for-the-civil-pe-exam-a-realistic-practice-exam-for-the-ncees-pe-civil-water-resources-and-environmental-exam/); [desertcart reviews](https://www.desertcart.nl/products/10527034-ppi-water-resources-and-environmental-depth-practice-exams-for-civil)). CEA's latest listed workshop dates from December 2023. Only PPI explicitly markets its January 2027 cohort as aligned with the April 2027 standards.

The complaints that surfaced point in the same direction: candidates cannot tell whether practice material is harder or easier than the real exam. One Engineer Boards poster scored 72% on EET's simulation and 84% on NCEES's; reviewers called PE Prepared "easier than actual exam" and said real wastewater questions were much harder than PPI's ([Engineer Boards](https://engineerboards.com/threads/what-pe-review-course-is-the-best-for-pe-civil-wre.28724/); [desertcart PE Prepared](https://www.desertcart.com.cy/products/39128519-civil-pe-practice-exam-water-resources-depth-version-b)). This evidence is thin and mostly old. Reddit, where recent WRE candidates actually post, could not be reached, so current complaints about navigating the on-screen handbook are unconfirmed.

The low-cost flank is not empty, and that matters. CastorPrep claims more than 1,000 original PE Civil problems, full mock exams, an AI tutor and a free trial; open-exam-prep.com offers free WRE study guides and questions; PEwise sells a light review for $149 ([CastorPrep](https://castorprep.com/resources/pe-review-courses); [open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/practice/pe-water-resources)). None of their quality or handbook referencing was verified. They are the closest rivals to an FE for Raccoons PE product and should be tested hands-on before any build decision.

## The verdict: build a simulation-first wedge, not a second FE site

A PE WRE product is a reasonable next product, with conditions. The case for it is strong on fit. WRE is the largest and fastest-growing PE Civil exam, the realistic-simulation and big-bank tier is genuinely vacant below $695, the April 2027 standards change sends every candidate looking for current material at the same moment, employers will cover the cost, and the founder is himself a 2027 WRE candidate who works in the exam's heaviest topic area. The FE site also feeds it over time, because most FE Civil passers sit a PE about four years later.

The case against is about scale and effort. If FE for Raccoons won 10% of the roughly 6,600 yearly attempts, that would be about $32,000 a year at $49, $65,000 at $99, or $98,000 at $149. That is meaningful side income, not a company-changing sum. PE problems cost far more to write than FE problems: each one is a 6-minute design calculation, the exam uses item formats the current FE sim lacks (multiple select, drag and drop, numeric entry), solutions must point into the PE handbook and seven purchased standards with exact editions, and wastewater and drinking water treatment are deep specialties outside Jerson's daily drainage work. Credibility is a further hurdle. Jerson will not hold a PE until about 2029, so the product needs a licensed reviewer on content until he does. He is also writing this around a full-time Kiewit job.

Those constraints point to a specific shape. Lead with the paid **80-question timed simulation in the real two-half CBT format, with the new item types**, because that is where the gap is cleanest and where FE for Raccoons already has working machinery. Price it above the FE sim, in the $99 to $149 range, since PE buyers are employed, often reimbursed, and buying against a large raise, and issue receipts suitable for expense reports. Grow a free, handbook-referenced worked-problem bank behind it, starting with Jerson's own strengths (hydrology, open channel, closed conduit, sitework) and adding treatment content with a PE reviewer. Tag every standards-based problem by edition and launch around April 2027 with "aligned to the April 2027 standards" as the hook. A corporate seat program can wait until individual sales prove the product.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that the April 2027 "new exam" is not new content at all. The outline is frozen through roughly 2030 and only the reference shelf changes, which means a problem bank written now does not expire in six months, and the real work is teaching candidates to find answers inside seven PDFs they cannot bring with them. That is a skill no competitor teaches directly, and it is the one most likely to make a cheap simulation feel better than an expensive course.

The second insight is that Jerson's own exam and the product share a calendar. His license date is fixed by experience at mid 2029 no matter when he sits, so he can choose a sitting date that serves both goals: study through early 2027, write problems as he studies, and sit either in the lighter January to March window or just after April with the product as his own test bench. The open questions that should be answered before committing are hands-on: how good CastorPrep and open-exam-prep actually are, what Kiewit reimburses, and whether a licensed WRE engineer will review the treatment content.
