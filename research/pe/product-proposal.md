# PE Civil WRE as an FE for Raccoons product: proposal

Written 2026-10-30. Built on `reports/PE water resources exam and market.md`;
all numbers are cited there. This is a proposal, not a decision: nothing here
gets built without Jerson's yes.

## Recommendation in one paragraph

Build it, but narrowly, and in two steps that each earn the next:

1. **Now:** a free waitlist page and free WRE worked problems. Search takes
   months to warm up, and these teach us whether the demand shows up.
2. **About April 2027, only if the waitlist fills:** decide the paid piece
   then. A $99 practice exam on its own no longer stands (see "The gap"); it
   has to beat PEwise at $149 with a course included, so price near $49 or
   offer something they do not.

Jerson studying for this exact exam now is the content engine and the story.

## Why this is worth doing

- **The market:** WRE is now the largest of the five PE Civil exams, with about
  6,600 attempts in the last 12 months, up a third in two years.
- **Repeat takers:** about 1,400 a year, and only half pass. They are the most
  motivated buyers there are.
- **Who pays:** employers usually do, up to caps of about $1,000 to $1,500. A
  product under $150 slips under every cap, and an engineer can expense it without
  asking anyone.
- **The gap (corrected 2026-10-31):** the first version said no affordable
  computer-based WRE practice exam existed. That was wrong. A second pass
  through search results and the sellers' own pages found:
  - PEwise: $149 for 90 days buys two 80-question exams in a timed simulator,
    plus a full video course. It launched in February 2026.
  - CastorPrep: $29 a month for 2,000+ problems and 80-question mock exams.
  - Civil Engineering Academy: a simulator for $99.96.
  - School of PE question bank: $139 for a month.
  - NCEES's own practice exam: $59.95 (PDF).
  So a $99 single exam would cost more than a competitor's two exams plus a
  course. The cheap computer exam is not an open gap any more. What is still
  open: being completely free at the core, which is FE for Raccoons' whole
  position, and reaching FE passers who already use the site.
- **The timing:** the topic outline stays the same until at least 2029, so
  work done now does not go stale. Only the supplied codes change in April 2027.

## Why it is narrow, not a second FE site

- **Smaller audience:** the FE Civil sees about 26,500 attempts a year; the WRE
  depth about 6,600. A whole second free platform would not pay back.
- **Expensive problems:** PE depth problems have more steps and need checking
  by a licensed PE. One wrong answer in a paid PE exam costs more trust than
  one in a free FE drill.
- **Modest ceiling:** if 10% of yearly attempts buy, the yearly revenue is about:

  | Price | Yearly revenue |
  | --- | --- |
  | $49 | $32,000 |
  | $99 | $65,000 |
  | $149 | $98,000 |

  For comparison, the FE simulation collected $1,404 in the last 90 days.

## The plan, step by step

### Step 1: learn whether people want it (November 2026 to March 2027)

- **Waitlist page:** a public page at /pe-civil-water-resources saying what is
  coming, with a "tell me when it's ready" signup that collects an email only.
  It gets indexed early, so it ranks by April.
- **Free problems:** 10 to 15 free worked problems on it, written while Jerson
  studies, one per topic. Six samples are already drafted in
  `sample-problems-wre.md`.
- **Founder content:** a short post series, "Studying for the PE while working
  as a drainage engineer". Jerson's real study log, no pass-rate claims.
- **Go signal:** 150 waitlist signups by March 31, 2027. Below 50 means hold.
  Between 50 and 150, Jerson decides.

### Step 2: the paid practice exam (April to June 2027, only on a go)

- **Format:** 80 questions in two locked halves of about 40, timed on the real
  clock, including the newer question styles (pick-several, click-on-figure,
  type-a-number).
- **Coverage:** follows the April 2027 spec and its seven supplied documents.
- **Results by topic:** scored per topic on the same 12 areas as NCEES's own
  failure report, weakest first. The FE simulation does this already.
- **Price:** not $99 as first written. Competitors sell two exams plus a
  course for $149, or monthly access for $29. Decide in March 2027 from the
  waitlist; $49 is the starting point. The receipt carries a
  company-name field so it can be expensed.
- **Code:** reuse the FE simulation's engine, which already has the
  server-picked questions, answers saved by question, and submit that only adds.
  The engine is the cheap part; the questions are the cost.
- **Review:** a licensed PE reviews every question in the treatment topics
  before launch. Jerson is not licensed until about 2029, so this needs a
  reviewer (ask Dr. Ames or a Kiewit PE).

### Later, only if Step 2 sells

- Company packs, five or more seats with one invoice. School of PE and PPI both
  sell to firms and hide their prices.
- A second exam form, so repeat takers get new questions.

## Decisions only Jerson can make

1. Go or hold on Step 1, the waitlist and free problems page.
2. The name: "PE for Raccoons", or the PE living inside FE for Raccoons. The
   brand rule stands: no invented story for the name.
3. Who reviews the treatment questions.
4. Whether to tell his own study story publicly.

## Risks

- **Accuracy:** PE questions are harder to get right. Mitigation: a computed
  check on every answer (as in the samples) and a licensed PE's review.
- **Focus:** this splits attention from FE growth. The FE search plan now runs
  itself every Monday, which is what makes room for this.
- **Copyright:** the NCEES handbook and the supplied codes are copyrighted. We
  point to sections and never copy them, the same rule as the NCEES FE practice
  exam.
- **Candidate voice:** Chrome cannot open Reddit, but Google's result pages
  show its WRE threads (read 2026-10-31). What they say: about 3 months and
  200 to 300 hours is the common study load; several people passed using only
  the NCEES practice exam and a book; School of PE is the most named course;
  people compare the 2024 and 2026 NCEES practice exams. Full threads stayed
  unread.
