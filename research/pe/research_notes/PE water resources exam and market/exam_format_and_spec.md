# PE Civil: Water Resources and Environmental (WRE) exam: format, specification and references (as of 2026-10-30)

Primary sources used (all fetched 2026-10-30):
- NCEES PE Civil page: https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/
- WRE spec, current (design standards effective April 2024): https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf
- WRE spec, design standards effective April 2027 (PDF created 2026-09-03 per file metadata): https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf
- NCEES Examinee Guide (cover dated October 2026, filename "November-2026"): https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf (linked from https://ncees.org/examinee-guide/)

Secondary sources (prep providers, professional societies, state boards) are labeled SECONDARY.

## Format: delivery, length, timing, question types, structure, availability, fee

### Takeaway
PE Civil WRE is a single 80-question, depth-only, computer-based exam: a 9-hour appointment with 8 hours of exam time and one optional 50-minute scheduled break. It is offered year-round at Pearson VUE centers, the fee is $400, and it mixes multiple choice with alternative item types. The old 40 breadth + 40 depth split ended in April 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Computer-based, administered year-round at NCEES-approved Pearson VUE test centers. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)
- 80 questions. Question types are multiple choice plus alternative item types (AITs). — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)
- The 9-hour appointment breaks down as a 2-min nondisclosure agreement, an 8-min tutorial, 8 hours of exam time and a 50-min scheduled break. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/); the same figures appear in the Examinee Guide schedule table ("PE Civil (all disciplines) Year-Round, 9 hr, 2 min, 8 min, 8 hr, 50 min, 80, SI, USCS"). — [Examinee Guide Oct 2026](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- The spec says: "Examinees have 9 hours to complete the exam, which contains 80 questions. The 9-hour time includes a tutorial and an optional scheduled break. Examinees work all questions." Units are SI and USCS. Questions call for "design, analysis, and application." — [WRE spec April 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- The exam is two sections, but the whole exam time is given at the start and neither section is timed on its own. After about half the questions, the examinee must review and submit them, loses access to them, and may then take the scheduled break. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Breaks: any unused scheduled break time does not carry over to the exam clock, and examinees may leave the building during the scheduled break. Unscheduled breaks are allowed but come out of testing time, and examinees may not leave the building. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- The alternative item types are: multiple correct options; point and click; drag and drop (match/sort/rank/label); and fill in the blank (numeric entry). Only PE Structural breadth is pure multiple choice, so every other exam, PE Civil WRE included, uses both MC and AITs. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Every item is scored right or wrong, with no partial credit and no penalty for wrong answers. A limited number of unscored pretest items are randomly placed in the exam and cannot be identified. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Every examinee tests on a 24-inch monitor sized for the question and the handbook side by side. One NCEES-approved calculator is allowed (see the NCEES Calculator Policy), and the test center supplies reusable booklets and markers. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Breadth vs depth: as of April 2024 all five PE Civil exams test only the depth content of their discipline. The standalone breadth portion is gone, but the exam is still 80 questions, 9 hours and CBT. — SECONDARY: [ASCE Civil Engineering Source, 2024-03-14](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2024/03/14/get-ready-for-an-all-new-pe-exam-format-heres-what-you-need-to-know), [NSPE](https://www.nspe.org/resources/licensure/resources/navigating-changes-the-civil-pe-exam-what-nspe-members-need-know), [Texas CE](https://www.texasce.org/tce-news/new-civil-pe-exam-standards/). The current NCEES WRE spec agrees: its one outline of 12 knowledge areas has no breadth/depth split. — [WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- Fee: $400 per attempt, paid to NCEES. Some licensing boards add their own application and fee. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/); [Examinee Guide fee table](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Related costs: Pearson charges $50 to reschedule or cancel an appointment. Cancelling the NCEES registration refunds the fee minus a $50 administrative fee (waived if a Pearson cancellation fee was already paid). Examinees must test within 12 months of board approval or forfeit the fee. Appointments must be made at least one business day ahead. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Testing windows (they matter for retakes) are Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep and Oct–Dec. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- With 8 hours for 80 questions, the pace is 6 minutes per question. Because the first half is locked once submitted, the realistic plan is about 4 hours for each block of about 40 questions.
- A credible prep product's practice exams need AITs (multi-select, drag-and-drop, numeric entry, point-and-click) with no partial credit, not MC alone.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives the exact share of AITs or the number of pretest items on WRE. NCEES says only "most questions" are MC and pretest items are "limited."
- The NCEES Civil page states no separate "breadth/depth ended April 2024" sentence. That date comes from SECONDARY sources plus the structure of the current spec.

## Content specification: WRE knowledge areas, question ranges, subtopics, effective dates, future change

### Takeaway
The current WRE outline has been in effect since April 2024: 12 knowledge areas, 80 questions. The spec NCEES posted for April 2027 keeps the outline word for word and changes only the list of supplied design standards. The heaviest areas are Project Sitework (9–14), Hydrology (8–12), and Closed Conduit, Open Channel and Wastewater (7–11 each).

### Cited Findings
The knowledge areas, question ranges and subtopics below are transcribed verbatim from the [WRE spec, effective April 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf). They match the [April 2027 WRE spec PDF](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf) exactly: a text diff of the two PDFs shows differences only on the design-standards page.

1. **Project Planning, 4–6**: A. Quantity take-off methods; B. Cost estimating; C. Project schedules; D. Activity identification and sequencing; E. Economic and sustainability analysis (e.g., present worth, lifecycle costs, comparison of alternatives)
2. **Soil Mechanics, 3–5**: A. Lateral earth pressure; B. Soil consolidation and compaction; C. Bearing capacity; D. Settlement; E. Slope stability
3. **Materials, 4–6**: A. Soil classification and boring log interpretation; B. Soil properties (strength, permeability, compressibility, phase relationships); C. Concrete (nonreinforced, reinforced); D. Piping materials; E. Material test methods and specification conformance
4. **Analysis and Design, 6–9**: A. Mass balance; B. Hydraulic loading; C. Solids loading (sediment loading, sludge); D. Hydraulic flow measurement

Pipe network analysis (series, parallel, loop networks)

Rainfall and stream gauging stations; G. Depletions (evaporation, detention, percolation, diversions); H. 8. **Groundwater and Wells, 4–6**: A. Aquifers; B. Groundwater flow; C. Well and drawdown analysis
9. **Surface Water and Groundwater Quality, 5–8**: A. Stream degradation and oxygen dynamics; B. Total maximum daily load (TMDL) (nutrient contamination, DO, load allocation); C. Biological and chemical contaminants
10. **Drinking Water Distribution and Treatment, 6–9**: A. Drinking water distribution systems; B. Drinking water treatment processes; C. Present, short-term, and long-term demands; D. Storage; E. 12. **Project Sitework, 9–14**: A. Excavation and embankment (grading, cut and fill); B. Construction site layout and control; C. - Effective dates: the current spec is labeled "Effective Beginning April 2024." The 2027 PDF still carries "Effective Beginning April 2024" on its knowledge-area page, but its design-standards page reads "Effective Beginning with the April 2027 Examination." — [current](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf); [2027](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf)
- NCEES lists the April 2024 specs as "effective before April 2027" and the new set as "effective beginning April 2027 (updated design standards)." Specs are posted six months before they take effect and change "once every 5–7 years," while design standards change more often. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)
- The current exam and current standards continue through March 31, 2027, and the updated standards take effect April 1, 2027. Topics, 80 questions, 9 hours and closed-book CBT are unchanged. — SECONDARY: [PPI2Pass](https://ppi2pass.com/resources/pe-exam/pe-civil-exam-changes)

### Inferences
- The question ranges add up to 70 at the minimum and 108 at the maximum. Scaling the midpoints (89 total) to 80 gives an approximate expected count per area: Project Sitework ~10.4, Hydrology ~9.0, Closed Conduit ~8.1, Open Channel ~8.1, Wastewater ~8.1, Analysis and Design ~6.7, Drinking Water ~6.7, Surface/Groundwater Quality ~5.8, Project Planning ~4.5, Materials ~4.5, Groundwater and Wells ~4.5, Soil Mechanics ~3.6. These are estimates, not NCEES figures.
- Grouped: water hydraulics and hydrology (areas 4–8) are about 36 of 80 questions, environmental treatment and quality (9–11) about 21, and civil "general" content (planning, soils, materials, sitework) about 23. The general content remains a large share even without the old breadth section.
- No new spec is announced beyond April 2027, so a product built on this outline should stay valid until about 2029–2031 (based on NCEES's "every 5–7 years" statement; UNVERIFIED as a date).

### Gaps
- The 2027 PDF filename ends in "-2" and was created 2026-09-03, which suggests an earlier posting was revised. What changed between postings could not be determined.

## Allowed references: handbook, design standards, open/closed book

### Takeaway
The exam is closed book with electronic references. Examinees get only the on-screen NCEES PE Civil Reference Handbook plus the design standards listed on the spec's last page. For WRE that is two GLUMRB "Ten States Standards" today, growing to seven documents in April 2027. The handbook version number could not be confirmed on an NCEES page.

### Cited Findings
- The spec says: "The exam is computer-based. It is closed book with electronic references. The NCEES PE Civil Reference Handbook is included in the exam along with the design standards shown on the last page." — [WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- NCEES supplies the electronic handbook and the listed design standards during the exam. These are the only permitted references, and personal copies are not allowed. NCEES does not sell printed handbooks or design standards. The handbook is available free through MyNCEES. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)
- The handbook appears on screen as a searchable PDF, and the exam always uses the current version. NCEES is licensed to provide design standard PDFs during the exam only, so examinees must buy them from the publishers to study. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Standards appear as searchable, linked PDFs, split into individual chapters, and only one chapter can be open and searched at a time. The handbook and standards are available for the whole exam. "Solutions to exam questions that reference a standard of practice are scored based on this list and the revision year shown. Solutions based on other standards will not receive credit." — [WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- **WRE design standards in effect through March 2027:** (1) TSS Wastewater Facilities 2014, *Recommended Standards for Wastewater Facilities*, Great Lakes–Upper Mississippi River Board (GLUMRB); (2) TSS Water Works 2018, *Recommended Standards for Water Works*, GLUMRB. — [WRE spec April 2024](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- **WRE design standards effective April 2027:**
  - CFR Title 29 Part 1926 (OSHA construction safety, 2024), Subparts B, C, D, E (1926.95–.107), F, G, H, M (Fall Protection 1926.500–.503 with App. A–E), P (Excavations 1926.650–.652 with App. A–F), S, Z, AA
  - USACE EM 1110-2-1902, *Slope Stability* (2003)
  - FHWA HIF-12-026, *Hydraulic Design of Highway Culverts*, HDS-5, 3rd ed., April 2012
  - FHWA NHI-06-086 / HEC-14, *Hydraulic Design of Energy Dissipators for Culverts and Channels*, 3rd ed., July 2006
  - TSS Wastewater Facilities 2014 (unchanged)
  - TSS Water Works **2022** (was 2018)
  - UFC 3-220-05, *Dewatering and Groundwater Control* (2004)

  — [WRE spec April 2027](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf)
- HEC-22 (urban drainage) is not on the current or the 2027 WRE list. — [current](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf); [2027](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- In April 2027 WRE study gains culvert design (HDS-5), energy dissipators (HEC-14), OSHA 1926 excavation and fall protection, slope stability (EM 1110-2-1902) and dewatering (UFC 3-220-05). These match subtopics already in the spec (6B, 6C, 12C, 12E, 2E). A prep product should teach how to find answers inside these PDFs and key its answers to the stated editions.
- Candidates testing Jan–Mar 2027 use the 2018 Water Works edition. From April 2027 they use 2022. Practice content citing Water Works needs version tags for each edition.

## Scoring, results, retakes

### Takeaway
Results are pass/fail and usually arrive 7–10 days after the exam. The score is the number correct converted to a scaled score, and the cut score is not published. Failing candidates get a diagnostic report scoring each knowledge area 0–15 against the average of passing examinees. Candidates may attempt the exam once per quarterly window and at most three times in any 12 months, and boards may be stricter.

### Cited Findings
- Results usually come in 7–10 days, by email and in MyNCEES. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/); [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- "All results are reported as pass or fail." Failing examinees receive a diagnostic report covering each major topic. NCEES does not manually verify CBT answers and does not release questions or answers. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- The score is the number correct, with no deductions for wrong answers, converted to a scaled score that adjusts for differences between forms. The passing level is set by subject-matter experts using psychometric methods and is not published. There is no predetermined pass percentage, and first-time and repeat takers are graded to the same standard. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- The diagnostic report scores each knowledge area on a 0–15 scale and compares it with the average of passing examinees. The number correct cannot be worked out from it. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- Retakes: one attempt per testing window (Jan–Mar, Apr–Jun, Jul–Sep, Oct–Dec) and no more than three in any 12-month period. Some boards are more restrictive. — [Examinee Guide](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NCEESExamGuide_November-2026.pdf)
- WRE pass rates as of July 2026: first-time takers 71% (n=2,620), repeat takers 49% (n=876). These are the highest of the five Civil disciplines and the largest first-time volume (Transportation 61%/1,929; Structural 62%/1,624; Construction 60%/1,000; Geotechnical 63%/434). — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/)

### Inferences
- WRE has the most first-time takers of any PE Civil discipline, about 2,620 in NCEES's reporting period (the period length was not shown), which makes it the largest Civil audience for a prep product.
- The 0–15 diagnostic by knowledge area is a natural reference for a prep product's readiness report, using the same 12 areas.

### Gaps
- The reporting period behind the July 2026 pass-rate figures (12 months? calendar year?) was not stated in the fetched page content.
- The passing scaled score is not published, by design.

## Changes in the last ~3–5 years

### Takeaway
PE Civil moved from paper to year-round CBT in April 2022 (last paper exam October 2021). That move also replaced the open-book exam with a closed-book exam using only NCEES-supplied electronic references. In April 2024 the breadth + depth format became depth-only discipline exams, still 80 questions and 9 hours. The next change, in April 2027, updates the design standards only.

### Cited Findings
- HISTORICAL: the last paper PE Civil exam was October 2021. CBT registration opened November 1, 2021, and year-round CBT appointments began April 1, 2022. NCEES had planned the switch for 2023 and moved it up because of COVID-19. — SECONDARY (state boards / prep): [NCBELS](https://www.ncbels.org/?p=1135), [Guam PEALS](https://peals.guam.gov/node/97), [FBPE](https://fbpe.org/pe-exams-moving-to-computer-based-testing-early/)
- HISTORICAL: the paper exam was open book (bound references allowed). The CBT exam is closed book with the NCEES handbook and supplied codes on screen. — SECONDARY: [ASCE Source, 2024-03-14](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2024/03/14/get-ready-for-an-all-new-pe-exam-format-heres-what-you-need-to-know). The current state is confirmed primary: "closed book with electronic references." — [WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- HISTORICAL: before April 2024 the PE Civil exam had a breadth segment common to all disciplines and a depth segment in the chosen discipline (traditionally 40 + 40). From April 2024 there are five separate depth-only exams. — SECONDARY: [ASCE](https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2024/03/14/get-ready-for-an-all-new-pe-exam-format-heres-what-you-need-to-know), [NSPE](https://www.nspe.org/resources/licensure/resources/navigating-changes-the-civil-pe-exam-what-nspe-members-need-know). Note: ASCE's article dates CBT to "spring of 2021," which conflicts with the board notices (April 2022). The board notices are more specific and more likely correct.
- Content brought over from the old breadth section (planning, soil mechanics, materials, sitework) stays in the WRE spec as areas 1–3 and 12. — [WRE spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Civ-WRE-April-202473.pdf)
- UPCOMING: on April 1, 2027 the design standards update (WRE goes from 2 standards to 7, and Water Works moves from 2018 to 2022), with the outline unchanged. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/exams/pe-exam/civil/); [WRE 2027 spec](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/PE-Civil-WRE-2027-Specs-2.pdf); SECONDARY [PPI2Pass](https://ppi2pass.com/resources/pe-exam/pe-civil-exam-changes)

### Inferences
- Paper-era books and question banks (open-book, breadth/depth, many outside codes) are out of date for structure. Prep material dated before April 2024 probably misweights content.

- UNVERIFIED: the exact pre-2024 WRE breadth/depth question split (40/40) for the CBT era; the older spec PDF was not retrieved.
