# PE Civil eligibility and licensure path, Washington (primary) and Oregon (secondary)

Subject: FE Civil passed around March 2025. BS civil (assumed ABET EAC) and MS civil (conferred April 2026), both BYU. Drainage engineer at Kiewit, Vancouver WA, since May 2026, working under licensed PEs. Research date: 2026-10-30.

Big picture: both states now let him take the PE before he has the experience. He registers straight with NCEES, with no board approval. The license itself still waits on experience. The earliest realistic license date in either state is around spring 2029, unless the board gives more credit than expected for the MS or for engineering work done before May 2026.

## 1. Washington: can he take the PE now? Is board approval needed?

### Takeaway
Yes. As of August 1, 2026, Washington has "decoupled" the exams from experience. He registers for the PE Civil directly with NCEES, needs no BRPELS approval, and can sit before finishing his experience. He applies to BRPELS only after he has passed the PE and meets every licensure requirement.

### Cited Findings
- BRPELS notice (July 8, 2026), "Exam Changes Coming": the Board no longer accepts exam applications, and board approval is no longer required to sit for an NCEES exam. Candidates register directly with NCEES for the FE, PE, SE, FS and PS. Decoupling means "separating experience from the exam" and "does not eliminate the experience requirement for becoming licensed." The notice also says "do not apply to BRPELS until you pass the NCEES exams AND meet our qualifications." — [BRPELS, Exam Changes Coming](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2026/exam-changes-coming)
- Web search summary of the same notice: the Board office stopped accepting exam applications on July 20, 2026, and direct NCEES registration began August 1, 2026. — [BRPELS, Exam Changes Coming](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2026/exam-changes-coming) (the exact dates come from the search-result summary of this page)
- Rulemaking order WSR 26-14-074 takes effect August 1, 2026 (adopted June 25, 2026, filed June 29, 2026, authority RCW 18.43.035). It carries out the 2026 amendments to chapter 18.43 RCW. It lets the board separate PE exam timing from the experience requirement, although experience must still be met before licensure. It removes the former board-approval-to-sit language from WAC 196-12-014 and repeals WAC 196-12-013. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- Board minutes from 2024 recorded a conflict between decoupling and the RCW 18.43 wording that required 8 years of experience before the exam. The 2026 statutory amendments resolved it. — [BRPELS 12-12-24 board minutes](https://www.brpels.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/12-12-24%20SBM%20Minutes.pdf) (summarized in search results; PDF not read directly)
- The PE-by-exam page says: "Board approval is no longer required to take an NCEES exam"; "DO NOT APPLY WITH BRPELS UNTIL YOU HAVE MET ALL REQUIREMENTS." — [BRPELS, Professional Engineer by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- The NCEES PE Civil exam is computer-based and given year-round at Pearson VUE centers. The five Civil depths (Construction, Geotechnical, Structural, Transportation, Water Resources and Environmental) are all year-round CBT. — [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/pe-exam/civil/)

### Inferences
- He can register with NCEES today for PE Civil: Water Resources and Environmental (the depth that fits drainage work), name Washington as his board, and book a Pearson seat. Seat availability usually runs weeks rather than months, but that is UNVERIFIED for the Portland/Vancouver centers.
- Passing the PE early has no downside under the new rules. The pass simply sits in his NCEES record until his experience is complete.

### Gaps
- The amended RCW 18.43.040 text was not retrieved. The version fetched from app.leg.wa.gov still read "broad based and progressive" and appears to predate the 2026 bill. The 2026 session law number was not identified.
- BRPELS links a "Decoupling Flowchart" whose contents were not read.

## 2. Washington: years of experience, MS credit, supervision, references

### Takeaway
Washington requires 8 years of combined education and experience. An ABET EAC BS counts as 4 of them. Credit for the MS is at the board's discretion, with no fixed amount in the 2026 rules. That leaves about 3 to 4 years of qualifying work, which must be verified by a PE who directly supervised him. Washington does not use a separate reference count. It uses experience verification by a supervising PE, and the NCEES Record is the preferred way to submit it.

### Cited Findings
- WAC 196-12-010 (as amended effective 2026-08-01): 8 years of engineering experience "satisfactory to the board," which may combine education and practical work. The former "broad based and progressive" wording was removed. Other requirements: the NCEES FE, the NCEES PE, the Washington engineer law review exam, a completed application and fees. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- WAC 196-12-021 (amended): an ABET EAC bachelor's in engineering equals 4 of the 8 years. Postgraduate engineering courses may earn experience credit "at the board's discretion," and the former one-year cap was removed. Official transcripts must be uploaded to the applicant's NCEES profile. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- WAC 196-12-020 (amended): experience must be verified by a licensed engineer in a board-approved jurisdiction, or as the board otherwise approves. The verifier must have directly supervised the applicant's technical work and know it personally. Experience gained during a postgraduate engineering program may count at the board's discretion, with no more than 1 year credited per calendar year. Applied research counts if done under a PE's guidance and documented. College-level teaching can count for up to 2 years. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- The BRPELS PE page describes the requirement as "8 years of progressive engineering experience working under a licensed PE," verified by a PE who directly supervised the work. The page still uses the older "progressive" wording. — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- Statute baseline (pre-amendment text as fetched): RCW 18.43.040(1) requires 8+ years, with an approved undergraduate engineering degree counting as 4. Postgraduate study and teaching may count as the board defines. — [RCW 18.43.040](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=18.43.040)
- Submission methods. The NCEES Record is the "Preferred method" and speeds up review. It is free to create, with a fee each time it is transmitted, and "Transmitting an NCEES record does not generate automatic approval." The alternative is the board's Work Experience Form plus Experience Verification Form, with transcripts sent to NCEES; that route "may increase review times." — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- The PE-by-exam page does not mention a number of references. — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)

### Inferences
- Likely credit: 4 years for the BS, plus perhaps 1 year for the MS. One year was the old capped amount, and the board may now give more or less. That totals about 5 years, leaving about 3 years of supervised work from May 2026, so about May 2029.
- If he did engineering work under a PE during the MS (an RA or co-op, for example), it may also count, at most 1 year per calendar year. It cannot overlap with MS coursework credit for the same period unless the board allows it (UNVERIFIED). Any pre-May 2026 internships under a PE could shorten the timeline.
- Under the new WAC 196-12-020, his Kiewit supervising PE(s) are the verifiers. Their licenses do not have to be Washington licenses, only from a "board-approved jurisdiction."

### Gaps
- No BRPELS guidance gives a number for MS credit under the new rules. The board will decide case by case, so this is UNVERIFIED. The pre-2026 cap was 1 year.
- Whether 2026 practice counts experience before the degree date was not confirmed. The "PE Best Practices.pdf" was not read.

## 3. Washington: EIT certificate, and where the FE was passed

### Takeaway
Washington does not require an EIT certificate for a PE license. The PE rule asks only that he passed the NCEES FE. An FE passed through another state is accepted through NCEES exam verification. A Washington EIT certificate is optional and cheap ($30). With an ABET BS he qualifies for it directly, but it does not change the PE path.

### Cited Findings
- WAC 196-12-010 lists passing the NCEES FE (or an equivalent) among the PE requirements. It does not list an EIT certificate. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- The PE application checklist says to verify passing the FE/EIT and PE exams, and for exams taken in another state, to request verification from NCEES or that state's board. — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- EIT eligibility: pass the FE and hold either a bachelor's in an ABET or CEAB accredited engineering curriculum or 4 years of experience. Apply online through SAW (Secure Access Washington) and then the BRPELS service. — [BRPELS, EIT certificate](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/engineering/engineer-training-certificate)
- Older BRPELS guidance said the EIT certificate was available only to applicants who designated Washington as their state when registering for the FE. The 2026 proposal WSR 26-11-036 repeals WAC 196-20-010 and 196-20-045 (the old FE registration and how-to-get-EIT sections). It allows the FE to be taken at any time and requires FE + 4 years (an EAC/ABET BS satisfies this) + application and fee for an EIT. — [WSR 26-11-036 (proposed)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/11/26-11-036.htm); designation rule per search summary of [BRPELS EIT page](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/119)
- A BRPELS FAQ says that someone who passed the FE in another jurisdiction can work in Washington as an EIT under the direct supervision of a PE. — search summary of [BRPELS licensing FAQ](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/faq) (FAQ not read directly, UNVERIFIED wording)
- EIT certificate fee: $30. — [BRPELS fees](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/fees)

### Inferences
- If he took the FE through Utah or another state, nothing needs redoing. NCEES transmits the FE result with his Record when he applies for the PE.

### Gaps
- Final adoption of WSR 26-11-036 was not confirmed. A related filing, WSR 26-14-076 (amending WAC 196-20-007), suggests it was adopted in July 2026: [WSR 26-14-076](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsrpdf/2026/14/26-14-076.pdf). UNVERIFIED.
- Which state his FE was registered under is unknown. Check his NCEES account.

## 4. Washington: state-specific exams, fees, processing times

### Takeaway
The one Washington-specific requirement is the Washington Engineer Law Review. It is an open-book form exam with an 80% pass mark, submitted with the license application, and there is no separate fee. The Washington application fee is $65. NCEES charges its own exam fee, reported by third parties as $400 (UNVERIFIED). Licenses are issued in the first week of each month. There is a licensing-system transition in early 2027.

### Cited Findings
- The Washington engineer law review exam is required for PE licensure. — [WSR 26-14-074, WAC 196-12-010](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- The application checklist says to upload the Law Review certificate: "Passing score is 80%." It also says "law reviews received prior to application will not be accepted." — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319) (the second quote comes from the search summary of the same page)
- The Law Review is a fillable PDF (form 166-031) to submit with the online application or by mail. Retakes can be emailed to engineers@brpels.wa.gov. — [BRPELS PE Law Review form](https://www.brpels.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-08/166-031%20PE%20Law%20Review.pdf) (summarized in search results)
- Fees: PE initial registration application $65, license print $5, EIT certificate $30, PE renewal (2 years) $128. NCEES exam fees are paid directly to NCEES, and fees are nonrefundable. — [BRPELS fees](https://brpels.wa.gov/licensing/fees)
- NCEES PE fee: $400 according to 2026 third-party guides. An older guide says $425. — [open-exam-prep PE Civil Transportation guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/blog/pe-civil-transportation-exam-guide-2026). UNVERIFIED on ncees.org.
- Processing: "If you applied and it has been more than 4 weeks please contact our office." "Licenses are issued the first week of every month." — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- EIT applications take up to 8 weeks to process. — search summary of [BRPELS EIT page](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/119)
- Transition notice: applications, renewals and other transactions will be limited during the move to a new licensing system in early 2027. Examinees who do not pass by January 15, 2027 may need to submit a new application. This seems to apply to old board exam approvals. — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)
- Applicants must provide all requested documentation in time, or the application may be abandoned and fees forfeited. — [BRPELS, Exam Changes Coming](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2026/exam-changes-coming)

### Inferences
- The January 15, 2027 note is aimed at people holding pre-decoupling board approvals. It should not affect someone who registers directly with NCEES now. UNVERIFIED.
- Total Washington out-of-pocket cost: about $400 to NCEES + $65 + $5 + the NCEES Record transmittal fee (amount not found) + an optional $30 EIT.

### Gaps
- Typical end-to-end review time for a full PE application was not published beyond the 4-week contact threshold.
- The NCEES Record transmittal fee and the official NCEES PE fee were not verified on ncees.org.

## 5. Oregon (OSBEELS), briefly

### Takeaway
Oregon has been decoupled since 2015 and has no board approval to sit, so he can take the PE now through NCEES. Licensure needs an ABET BS plus 4 years of supervised experience. A post-baccalaureate engineering degree replaces 1 year, so he needs 3 years of work, about May 2029. Oregon requires 5 references, at least 3 of them PEs, and the NCEES Record can supply them. No EI certificate and no Oregon law exam for civil PEs were found in the rule.

### Cited Findings
- OSBEELS newsletter (2022): the Board's 2015 changes "decoupled the exam process." Experience is not required before exams. — [Oregon Examiner, Spring 2022](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/Documents/OregonExaminer/2022Examiner-Spring.pdf). A 2021 surveyor article dates Oregon's decoupling to 2005 instead: [PLSO Oregon Surveyor Sep/Oct 2021](https://associationpublications.com/flipbook/plso/2021/SeptOct/2). The board's own 2015 date is more authoritative.
- With minor exceptions (the Oregon-specific exams), OSBEELS has no involvement in the national exam process. Candidates coordinate the FE and PE with NCEES. — [PLSO Oregon Surveyor Mar/Apr 2023](https://associationpublications.com/flipbook/plso/2023/MarApr/10)
- OAR 820-010-1020(1): an accredited (EAC/ETAC/ACCE) engineering bachelor's + 4 years of experience. A post-baccalaureate engineering degree may replace 1 year. Teaching plus graduate credit combined cap at 2 years. Experience must be active engineering practice under the direction and supervision of a registered PE. — [OAR 820-010-1020](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_820-010-1020)
- OAR 820-010-1000 requirements: pass the NCEES FE and an NCEES PE in a recognized branch, have a Board-approved education/experience combination, submit transcripts and an Experience Details form, and provide 5 references. At least 3 references must be active PEs in an NCEES jurisdiction, and all work must be verified by at least one reference who directly supervised it. The NCEES Record may be released in place of the exam, transcript, experience and reference items. EI certification is not listed as a prerequisite. The Oregon-specific exams are for Acoustical and Forest engineering only. — [OAR 820-010-1000](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_820-010-1000)
- The NCEES Record can supply the education, exam, references and experience sections, and the rest of the application must still be completed. NCEES may charge a transmittal fee. — [OSBEELS Applications](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/obtaining/Pages/Applications.aspx)
- Applications are filed online through MyOSBEELS. Registration Specialists review them and recommend approval at the next Board meeting, under a published deadline schedule. — [OSBEELS Obtaining a license](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/obtaining/pages/default.aspx)
- The fee schedule is in OAR 820-080-0010, and fees are nonrefundable. — [OSBEELS 2019 Registration Application](https://www.oregon.gov/osbeels/Documents/Form/201912_RegistrationApplication.pdf)

### Inferences
- Oregon's math is more certain than Washington's, because the 1-year MS substitution is written into the rule: 4 years − 1 year = 3 years of work from May 2026, so about May 2029. Washington needs a board-discretion MS credit to land the same date.
- Getting both licenses: one PE pass, one NCEES Record transmitted to both boards. After the first license, comity is another route (Washington comity fee $110).
- Oregon's 5 references (3 of them PEs) are a heavier ask than Washington's verification by a supervising PE. He should start collecting them early, from Kiewit PEs and BYU faculty who are PEs.

### Gaps
- Current Oregon fee amounts in OAR 820-080-0010 were not retrieved.
- Whether Oregon requires any law or ethics exam for civil PEs: none appears in OAR 820-010-1000, but the "Licensing Exam Information" page was not read. Treat "no Oregon law exam" as UNVERIFIED.
- The Board meeting cadence and deadline table (which sets Oregon approval timing) were not retrieved.

## 6. Practical timeline for this engineer

### Takeaway
He can take the PE as soon as he is ready. A realistic first sitting is early to mid 2027, after a few months of study. The earliest license in either state is about May to June 2029, from 3 years of Kiewit experience on top of the BS + MS credit. It could come earlier only if the board credits pre-May 2026 supervised work or gives more than 1 year for the MS.

### Cited Findings
- Exams are decoupled and booked directly with NCEES, and the PE Civil is offered year-round. — [BRPELS, Exam Changes Coming](https://brpels.wa.gov/news/2026/exam-changes-coming); [NCEES PE Civil](https://ncees.org/pe-exam/civil/)
- Washington: 8 years total, the ABET BS = 4, MS credit at board discretion. — [WSR 26-14-074](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/wsr/2026/14/26-14-074.htm)
- Oregon: ABET BS + 4 years, and a graduate degree replaces 1 year. — [OAR 820-010-1020](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_820-010-1020)
- Washington licenses are issued the first week of each month. — [BRPELS, PE by Exam](https://brpels.wa.gov/node/319)

### Inferences (step list)
1. Now: create or update the NCEES Record. Add the BYU BS and MS transcripts (sent to NCEES), confirm the FE result is on file, and start logging Kiewit experience with his supervising PE.
2. Now to early 2027: register with NCEES for PE Civil, Water Resources and Environmental, with Washington as the board. Study and sit. A 3 to 4 month study window is a common norm (UNVERIFIED).
3. Optional, any time: get the Washington EIT certificate ($30, FE + ABET BS). It is nice for a résumé or title but not required.
4. About May 2029 (3 years at Kiewit): Washington application through SAW/BRPELS ($65), law review exam (80% pass) submitted with it, and NCEES Record transmitted. The license arrives in the first week of a following month, so about June to July 2029.
5. Oregon in parallel or afterwards: MyOSBEELS application with the same NCEES Record including 5 references (3 PEs). Approval comes at the next Board meeting after the deadline.
- Risk: Washington's new licensing system in early 2027 may slow transactions. That matters only for an EIT application in that window.

### Gaps
- His actual pre-May 2026 engineering work (internships, an RA under a PE) is unknown. It is the main lever for an earlier license.
- No official source states how Washington will credit an MS under the new discretionary rule.
