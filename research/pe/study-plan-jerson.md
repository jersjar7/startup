# Jerson's PE Civil Water Resources and Environmental study plan

Written 2026-10-30 from the research in `reports/` and `research_notes/`.
Every fact here is cited in those notes.

## The decision that shapes everything

**Take the exam in March 2027, before the April 2027 change.**

- You can book it now. Washington stopped requiring experience before the PE
  exam on 2026-08-01. You register directly with NCEES and need no board
  approval first. Oregon has worked this way since 2015.
- In April 2027 NCEES keeps the same 12 topics but changes the codes and
  manuals it hands you inside the exam:

  | | Through March 2027 | From April 2027 |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Documents | 2 (the Ten States Standards for wastewater and for water works) | 7 (adds OSHA excavation rules, the USACE slope stability manual, FHWA culvert design (HDS-5), HEC-14 energy dissipators and the UFC dewatering manual) |
  | What it means for you | Less to learn your way around | Five more documents to know |

- Two of the new documents, HDS-5 and HEC-14, are drainage manuals you
  already use at Kiewit, so waiting is not all bad. But learning five extra
  documents is more work than the head start is worth.
- Fallback: if March slips, test in May or June 2027 under the new list.
  The topics stay the same, so all the study carries over. Only the code
  documents change.

## What the exam is

- 80 questions on a computer at a Pearson VUE center, any day of the year.
- 9-hour appointment: 8 hours of exam with an optional 50-minute break.
  Once you submit the first half (about 40 questions), you cannot go back to it.
- Closed book. You get the NCEES PE Civil Reference Handbook and the listed
  codes on screen, searchable, one chapter open at a time. Finding things fast
  in those PDFs is a skill to practise in its own right.
- Most questions are multiple choice. Some are pick-several, click-on-a-figure,
  drag-and-drop or type-a-number.
- Pass or fail, with results 7 to 10 days later. The fee is $400 (ask Kiewit
  to cover it).
- Of 2,620 first-time takers in early 2026, 71% passed. Of 876 repeat takers,
  49% passed. Plan to pass first time: retakes are limited to one per quarter.

About 30 questions sit in your strengths (hydrology, open channel, closed
conduit). About 25 sit in water and wastewater treatment and water quality,
which drainage work does not touch. **That treatment block is where the exam
will be won or lost for you.**

## Rules that make this work

- **Work problems, don't read.** Every study session is problems first. Read
  the theory only when a problem stops you. This is how FE for Raccoons
  teaches, and it works for you too.
- **Look up every formula in the handbook PDF**, never in notes. In the exam,
  the time you lose is lost finding things.
- **Keep a miss log**: each wrong answer, why you missed it and the handbook
  page. Re-work the log every Sunday.
- **Treatment topics early in the day.** Your drainage topics need less fresh
  attention.

## Licensure timeline

- **March 2027:** pass the PE.
- **Now until licensure:** log your Kiewit work in your NCEES Record every few
  months while you still remember the details.
- **About May 2029:** apply in Washington. You need 8 years in total, combining
  school and work. Your ABET bachelor's counts as 4. The board decides how much
  your master's counts (it used to be 1 year), leaving about 3 years of work.
  - You also need the Washington law review (a form, 80% to pass).
  - A licensed PE who supervised you must verify your experience.
- **Earlier is possible** if any MS research or internship counts as engineering
  experience under a PE. Worth asking the board.
- **Oregon in parallel:** your bachelor's plus 4 years under a PE, with the
  master's replacing 1 year, so also about 3 years of work. You need 5
  references, at least 3 of them PEs.
